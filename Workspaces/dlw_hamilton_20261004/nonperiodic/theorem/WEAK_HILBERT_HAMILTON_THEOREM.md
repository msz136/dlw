# A common-domain, densely defined Hamilton theorem

Independent audit, 2026-10-04. This gives a constructive replacement for a generic affine Schwartz phase space. It is an unbounded Hamilton differential equation on a moving affine finite-energy space, not a local-existence or invariant-domain theorem.

## 1. Reference and ambient phase space

Let `B(t)=(A(t),C(t))` be any smooth exact solution of the ORIGINAL semi-discrete equations

\[
d\{A_t+D(A^2/2+\beta C^2)+D^2A\}+MD^2C=0,
\qquad C_t=-D(AC)+D^2C,
\]

where `d=(1-E⁻¹)/h`, `M=(1+E⁻¹)/2`, `D=∂x`, `β=h²/32`, `h>0`. Assume boundedness of `A,C` and their x derivatives through the orders used below, locally uniformly in t. The intended paper benchmarks are allowed references whenever their tau denominators stay nonzero; they need not belong to a finite-total-energy space themselves.

Put

\[
X=\ell^2(\mathbb Z;L^2(\mathbb R))^2,
\qquad X_s=H^s(\mathbb R;\ell^2(\mathbb Z))^2,
\]

with real pairing `⟨f,g⟩=h∑∫fg`. The physical states are `B(t)+(p,r)`, `(p,r)∈X`. This is a time-dependent affine bundle because generally `B(t)-B(0)∉X` for extended line waves.

## 2. The self-adjoint nonlocal operator

Use the unitary Fourier transform in j and x, with variables `θ∈(-π,π]` and `ξ∈R`. Define the real multiplier

\[
m(\theta,\xi)=\frac{h\xi}{2}\cot(\theta/2),
\quad \operatorname{Dom}K=\{r\in\ell^2L^2:m\hat r\in L^2_{\theta,\xi}\},
\quad \widehat{Kr}=m\hat r.
\tag{1}
\]

Values at the null set `θ=0` do not matter. `K` is a densely defined self-adjoint real operator; it is the closed multiplier realization of `RD`, not a bounded inverse on all `ℓ²`. It commutes with x differentiation on the common domain and

\[
dKr=MDr.\tag{2}
\]

For joint Schwartz r, membership in `DomK` is equivalent to `D∑r_j=0`; x decay then gives `∑r_j=0`. Thus the spectral domain correctly detects the lattice-mass obstruction. More general admissible finite-energy perturbations need not have an absolutely convergent lattice sum at all; this is how this phase space differs from a two-moment Schwartz ansatz.

Define the dense graph domain

\[
Y=\{(p,r)\in X_1:r\in\operatorname{Dom}K\},
\qquad \|(p,r)\|_Y=\|(p,r)\|_{X_1}+\|Kr\|_{\ell^2L^2}.
\tag{3}
\]

Density follows by Fourier cutoffs away from `θ=0`, followed by smooth spectral approximation. In one continuous dimension, `ℓ²H¹_x` embeds in `L∞(Z×R)` and is a Banach multiplication algebra. Thus all polynomial terms below are well defined.

Moreover Y states have useful pointwise far-field control:

\[
\sup_x|p_j(x)|+\sup_x|r_j(x)|\longrightarrow0\quad(|j|\to\infty),
\]

and

\[
\sup_j|p_j(x)|+\sup_j|r_j(x)|\longrightarrow0\quad(|x|\to\infty).
\]

The first follows from one-dimensional Sobolev bounds site by site; the second from the vanishing at infinity of Hilbert-valued `H¹(R;ℓ²)` functions. These are properties of domain states. Invariance of Y under evolution is not asserted.

## 3. A finite relative Hamilton functional and its differential

Define directly, without subtracting divergent total energies,

\[
K_B(t;p,r)=h\sum_j\int\left[
\frac{C_j}2p_j^2+A_jp_jr_j+\frac12p_j^2r_j+
\beta C_jr_j^2+\frac\beta3r_j^3+r_jDp_j
\right]dx+\frac12\langle r,Kr\rangle.
\tag{4}
\]

It is a finite smooth real functional on Y with its graph topology. Its first differential is uniquely represented by an X covector, since Y is dense in X:

\[
\nabla_XK_B=\binom{Cp+Ar+pr-Dr}{Ap+p^2/2+2\beta Cr+\beta r^2+Dp+Kr}.
\tag{5}
\]

The last term follows from self-adjointness of K, rather than a boundary manipulation on an undefined background total integral. The x derivative term follows by Sobolev integration by parts. This is graph-domain differentiability, not differentiability of a continuous functional on all X.

No definition of `RDC` on the reference is needed: all nonlocal operations in (4)–(5) act only on the perturbation. This is a useful advantage over literal vacuum-subtraction.

## 4. Poisson operator and its exact domain

Define the skew-adjoint constant operator on X

\[
J_0=-\begin{pmatrix}0&D\\D&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \operatorname{Dom}J_0=X_1.
\tag{6}
\]

Let Φ be real smooth Fourier test functions with compact θ support away from zero and Schwartz x behavior, closed under D and K. It is dense in X. Smooth cylinder functionals generated by `⟨(p,r),φ⟩`, `φ∈Φ²`, form a Poisson algebra under

\[
\{F,G\}=\langle\nabla F,J_0\nabla G\rangle.\tag{7}
\]

The bracket is skew by integration by parts. Cylinder closure reduces (7) to a finite matrix of constants `⟨φ_a,J₀φ_b⟩`; its constant coefficients prove Jacobi exactly. This is a rigorous proof on a specified dense test-functional algebra.

Define the dynamic domain

\[
\mathcal D=\{(p,r)\in X_2:Kr\in H^1(\mathbb R;\ell^2)\}.
\tag{8}
\]

For bounded reference x derivatives, (5) lies in `DomJ₀` at every state in `D`, so `J₀∇K_B∈X`. Mixed brackets `{F,K_B}` are well defined there; alternatively `-⟨J₀∇F,∇K_B⟩` defines them on Y. Jacobi triples involving two test cylinder functionals and one K_B hold on D by symmetry of the graph-domain Hessian and because the test directions `J₀∇F,J₀∇G` belong to Y.

Do not claim that K_B lies in a closed Poisson algebra of all smooth X functionals: K_B is an unbounded Hamiltonian, and repeated mixed brackets require their own domain checks. The valid assertion is the Poisson cylinder algebra plus this specified unbounded Hamilton generator.

## 5. Equivalence theorem

**Theorem.** Let `B` satisfy Section 1, and let `(p,r)∈C¹(I;X)` with values in D and sufficient time continuity to interpret the displayed terms. Then the following statements are equivalent:

1. `B+(p,r)` solves the original semi-discrete equations, with derivatives interpreted in X after subtracting the reference equations.
2. `(p,r)_t=J₀∇_XK_B(t;p,r)` in X.

In components, the latter is

\[
p_t=-D(Ap+p^2/2+2\beta Cr+\beta r^2)-D^2p-DKr,
\]

\[
r_t=-D(Cp+Ar+pr)+D^2r.\tag{9}
\]

Proof: the second equation is immediate. Subtract the reference first equation from the full first equation and use (2); its residual is `dF=0`, where

\[
F=p_t+D(Ap+p^2/2+2\beta Cr+\beta r^2)+D^2p+DKr\in\ell^2L^2.
\]

The kernel of d on `ℓ²(Z;L²(R))` is zero: a lattice-constant nonzero vector is not square summable. Therefore `F=0`. Conversely multiplying (9) by d and using (2) restores the original difference first equation. There is no omitted mean gauge in this finite-energy perturbation sector.

For a time-dependent reference the equation is a nonautonomous Hamiltonian equation. It does not make K_B a conserved quantity. Neither local existence, invariant persistence of D/Y, nor a Liouville theorem follows from this representation.

## 6. Physical (u,v) coordinates

Let `δ₀=(E-E⁻¹)/(2h)` and use perturbation variables

\[
\delta u=p,\qquad \delta v=q=r+\delta_0p.
\]

The bounded invertible shear `S(p,r)=(p,r+δ₀p)` preserves `J₀`, since `δ₀*=-δ₀` and `[δ₀,D]=0`:

\[
S J_0S^*=J_0.
\]

Thus the physical perturbation Hamiltonian is (4) with `r=q-δ₀p`, on the transformed graph and dynamic domains. Its Poisson operator is still first order in x with lattice radius zero; the Hamiltonian K contributes infinite lattice range. The background coordinates are `u*=A-2a`, `v*=C+4+δ₀A`.

## 7. What this resolves and what remains

This theorem places the finite relative functional, operator domains, gradient, skewness, Jacobi test algebra and exact equation equivalence in one common set of assumptions. It is a legitimate densely defined weak Hamilton representation around nonperiodic benchmark backgrounds. It does not claim a Hamilton flow on all generic Schwartz neighbors; that stronger claim is contradicted by the moment obstruction recorded in `THEOREM_AUDIT.md`.

Future independent commuting charges must be finite on this moving affine graph domain and must satisfy the mixed-bracket domain conditions. In particular the unrenormalized total Hamiltonian, lattice masses not known to converge, and the mere existence of a Lax pair cannot supply that next step.
