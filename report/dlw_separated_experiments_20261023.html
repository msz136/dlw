Initial spatial defects and time integration

We use four exact soliton solutions to examine spatial approximation and time integration separately. Section 5.1 specifies the waves and the quantities measured. In Section 5.2, we evaluate the initial spatial defects without advancing in time, varying either the lattice spacing or the resolution in xxx. We then retain Centered-SD in Section 5.3 and compare the computed fields after time integration with three methods.

These initial comparisons distinguish the effect of the y formulation from the additional x approximation. Since no time stepping enters these measurements, differences between the time integrators can be examined separately.

The effect of the time integrator varies substantially between the tests. In A, RK4 and Crank–Nicolson give closely matched errors, while Euler gives slightly smaller final maxima. In B, the differences are much larger: the u error is about 0.075 with Euler, 0.163 with RK4 and 0.167 with Crank–Nicolson. These errors are much larger than the initial spatial defects in Section 5.2. The initial defect alone therefore does not explain the final error magnitude in this case.

In C, Euler gives the smallest measured u error and RK4 the smallest v error. The maximum differences between the Crank–Nicolson and RK4 fields are 1.485 × 10-6 in u and 4.217 × 10-7 in v. In D, Euler gives the smallest measured u error and RK4 the smallest v error. The maximum differences between the Crank–Nicolson and RK4 fields are 1.732 × 10-6 in u and 4.253 × 10-7 in v.

The final errors combine the spatial approximation with its subsequent numerical evolution. We interpret the fixed-step comparison as a measure of final-field accuracy and sensitivity to the time integrator; establishing temporal orders would require a separate step-size study. All Crank–Nicolson steps satisfy the prescribed implicit residual tolerance.
