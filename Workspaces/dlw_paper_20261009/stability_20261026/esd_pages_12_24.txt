Together with the second lattice equation this is an explicit method-of-lines evolution. No inverse periodic y
difference is used. All stencils are backward in k, so there is no right-boundary datum. The field vk belongs
to the edge (k −1, k); its midpoint is used in plots.
The x interval is periodic, [−96, 96), with Nx = 384 equally spaced points. Fourier collocation approx-
imates first and second x derivatives directly. The first derivative’s Nyquist coefficient is set to zero, as
appropriate for real trigonometric collocation; the second derivative retains its −ξ2 coefficient. Products
are evaluated pointwise, with no filtering, artificial viscosity, or dealiasing. The prescribed boundary flux
is differentiated by the same Fourier operator. Classical RK4 advances the resulting nonautonomous ODE,
evaluating the boundary at every stage. Forward Euler is used only for the time-order test.
