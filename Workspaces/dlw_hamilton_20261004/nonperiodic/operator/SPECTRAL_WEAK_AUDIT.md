# Audit of a densely defined relative Hamilton system

This extension avoids claiming invariance of the Schwartz zero-sum constraints. It is mathematically weaker than a physical, autonomous Hamilton theorem on a fixed open phase space.

Let `X=ℓ²(ℤ;L²(ℝ))`, with pairing `h∑j∫dx`, and Fourier variables `(θ,ξ)∈(−π,π)×ℝ`. Define the multiplication operator

\[
\widehat{Kf}(\theta,\xi)=m(\theta,\xi)\hat f(\theta,\xi),
\qquad m=\frac{h\xi}{2}\cot\frac\theta2,
\qquad \operatorname{Dom}K=\{f\in X:m\hat f\in L^2\}.
\]

Values on the measure-zero line `θ=0` are irrelevant. `K` is a densely defined self-adjoint operator, preserves real fields, and agrees with `RD` on the common summable zero-sum domain. `dK=MD` on `DomK∩DomD`. Unlike the symbol of `R`, `K` is a well-defined Hilbert-space operator without choosing values of a singular primitive at zero lattice frequency.

Let `B(t)=(U*,w*)` be any benchmark exact solution with bounded coefficients and bounded derivatives required below. Write `z=B(t)+y`, `y=(p,r)`. On

\[
Y=\{p\in\ell^2H_x^1,\ r\in\ell^2H_x^1\cap\operatorname{Dom}K\},
\]

with its graph norm, define the Bregman relative functional

\[
H_B(t;p,r)=h\sum_j\int\left[
\frac12w^*p^2+U^*pr+\frac12p^2r
+\beta w^*r^2+\frac\beta3r^3+rDp+\frac12rKr\right]dx.
\]

All integrals are absolutely convergent. The Sobolev embedding `ℓ²H¹_x→ℓ∞L∞_x` controls cubic terms. The functional is smooth in the graph norm, and its first derivative admits an `X`-gradient

\[
g_U=w^*p+U^*r+pr-Dr,
\qquad
g_w=U^*p+\frac12p^2+2\beta w^*r+\beta r^2+Dp+Kr.
\]

These are the differences of the formal full gradients at `B+y` and `B`, not the full gradients themselves.

The skew-adjoint constant operator

\[
J_0=-\begin{pmatrix}0&D\\D&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \operatorname{Dom}J_0=\ell^2H_x^1\oplus\ell^2H_x^1,
\]

defines a Hamilton vector field `J₀g` on the dense dynamic domain

\[
\mathcal D=\{p,r\in\ell^2H_x^2,\ r\in\operatorname{Dom}K,
\ Kr\in\ell^2H_x^1\}.
\]

The product terms have `H¹` regularity because background coefficients have bounded first derivatives and perturbations have `H²` regularity. The resulting equation is

\[
y_t=J_0\nabla_XH_B(t;y)=F(B+y)-F(B).
\]

Using `dK=MD` yields the difference of the original semi-discrete equations. Conversely their difference and the fixed background imply this vector field because `ker d∩X={0}`: a lattice-constant nonzero sequence cannot be in `ℓ²`. These statements are exact for sufficiently differentiable paths staying in `\mathcal D`; they do not assert existence, uniqueness, or preservation of `\mathcal D`.

## Poisson claim with its proper scope

Use real smooth test kernels whose Fourier transforms have compact `ξ` support and smooth `θ` support away from 0. They form a dense `J₀`-stable test space. Smooth cylinder functions generated by finitely many such kernels form a bracket-closed algebra:

\[
\{F,G\}=\langle\nabla F,J_0\nabla G\rangle_X.
\]

Each finite collection has a constant skew structure matrix, so Jacobi holds exactly. The relative Hamiltonian is not a cylinder function. Its bracket with a test cylinder is defined on the graph domain by moving `J₀` onto the test gradient. Mixed Jacobi identities with a single `H_B` follow on a sufficiently smooth dynamic domain from the symmetry of its Hessian; an arbitrary bracket-closed algebra containing `H_B` and repeated brackets has not been constructed. Multiplication by nonperiodic background coefficients can reintroduce lattice frequencies near `θ=0`, so repeated admissibility cannot be assumed.

## Far fields and the missing physical autonomous theorem

Every snapshot in `ℓ²H¹_x` belongs to `C₀(ℤ×ℝ)`: tails in `j` vanish uniformly in `x`, and tails in `x` vanish uniformly in `j`. This follows by finite-site approximation and the one-dimensional Sobolev embedding. Hence the energy domain retains the pointwise background far fields. It does not retain exponential rates or an absolutely summable lattice mass. Allowing nonsummable `ℓ²` tails is exactly what avoids the pointwise lattice-sum compatibility hierarchy.

Since `B` is time-dependent,

\[
z_t=B_t+J_0\nabla H_B.
\]

Thus this is a finite relative-coordinate Hamilton representation with an explicit reference drift, not the pure physical equation `z_t=J₀δH_B/δz`. In particular `H_B` is explicitly time-dependent and is not automatically conserved. A frozen reference cannot absorb `B_t` into an `X`-gradient: the line-soliton velocity itself is not in `X`, because it extends along infinitely many lattice sites. Obtaining an autonomous physical Hamilton theorem needs a larger phase space and an additional proved Poisson/reconstruction structure, or another justified renormalization. The present audit does not supply that step.
