The four tests distinguish the accuracy of the initial time derivatives from that of the fields after evolution. Endpoint-SD retains its initial advantage in the first component on the Gaussian and sinusoidal backgrounds, while Centered-SD gives smaller terminal errors than Endpoint-SD in both fields in the soliton tests. FE gives the smallest second-field error throughout the comparison, and its common first equation with FD makes the influence of the second-field approximation visible in their subsequent evolution. The initial approximation of one component must therefore be considered together with the evolution of the coupled fields.

The consistency analysis identifies the truncation terms introduced by each lattice arrangement. During evolution, the coupling provides a route for these errors to affect the other component; in particular, the vxxv_{xx}vxx​ term links the approximation of vvv to the computed uuu field. The change in error ordering in the soliton tests is consistent with this coupling. Initial derivative errors and evolved field errors thus provide complementary measures of the spatial approximation.
